Each product page can now look like its own product: its colour fills the main button, a page can wash in it, and three worked examples show three very different pages built on the same shared parts.

## Upgrading: what changes on your site

Two things change on upgrade without you doing anything:

- **A product page's main button takes the product's colour.** `.pp-button` on a page with `data-mark="teal"` (or `violet`, `magenta`) is now filled with that colour, with a white label, instead of blue. A product page with no `data-mark` gets an ink button. Secondary buttons, links and the focus ring look as before. Inside apps nothing changes: `.btn-primary` and links stay blue.

- **On a touch screen, a product page's header and footer links are 44px tall**, like its buttons already were. With a mouse they keep their tighter rows.

A product page's main button can't go back to blue, but `data-button="ink"` on `.pp` gives an ink button.

## Each product page looks like itself

Driftwood, Guidebook and Sites used to share one header, one background, one headline style, one blue button and one rounded stage, so they read as one site with the words swapped (ADR-0017). Now each product page picks five things, writes them under **Look** in its `BRIEF.md`, and no two products pick the same:

- **Ground:** the page's surface.
- **Voice:** how the headline is set.
- **Shape:** the corners.
- **Composition:** where the product sits on the first screen.
- **The page does the thing:** one piece of the product's own interface used on the page itself.

`DESIGN.md` §13 keeps a table of who picked what, so the next product can see what's taken.

`product-page.css` is now marked in two parts. The **shared** parts are what every page needs and nobody should rewrite: the skip link, the status, the buttons, the clip controls, the form and its states, prices and the legal line. The **starting point** parts are the header, hero, bands, close and footer layout, which a product overrides in its own stylesheet to build its look.

New attributes on `.pp`:

- `data-button="ink"`: fill the main button with ink instead of the product's colour.
- `data-ground="mark"`: wash the whole page in the product's colour.
- `data-theme="dark"` (or `light`): pin the page to one theme. Only when the look *is* the theme, like an always-dark console. Apps never pin.

## New tokens

For a product page only, never in an app:

- `--mark-fill`, `--mark-fill-hover`, `--mark-fill-pressed`, with `--on-mark` for the label: the main button in the product's colour. A white label passes 4.5:1 on every one, in both themes.
- `--mark-surface`: a faint, solid wash of the product's colour that body text and links read on.

`data-mark` points all of them at the product's colour, the way it does `--mark-current`. With no `data-mark` they are the family amber. Each product also has its own named set (`--mark-teal-fill`, `--mark-teal-surface` and so on). Tailwind consumers get `bg-mark-fill`, `bg-mark-surface`, `text-on-mark` and the hover and pressed steps.

## Three worked examples

- [Driftwood](https://design.brignano.io/previews/product-page-example.html): always dark, all mono, square. The system map is the page's background, and the last deploy shows as a log of diffs.
- [Guidebook](https://design.brignano.io/previews/product-page-guidebook.html): bright, very large heavy type, pills. A Guidebook step points at the page's own button, and the header counts the page's steps as you scroll.
- [Sites](https://design.brignano.io/previews/product-page-sites.html): a warm wash of its colour, soft type, round corners. The hero is a customer's text, the reply, and the site changing.

Each is the shared parts plus a short `<style>` block, in tokens only: copy the one closest to your product and write your own look over it. All three pass the first-screen check (`scripts/fold.mjs`).
