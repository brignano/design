Content guidelines

Voice

Competent, calm, plain words. Say what happened and what to do next, the way a colleague who knows the system would. No hype, no jokes, no apology theatre.

Do

Changes saved.

Don't

Awesome! Your changes have been successfully saved!

Sentence case

Everything is in sentence case: buttons, headings, labels, menu items, tabs, page titles. Capitalise only the first word and proper nouns. An eyebrow can be uppercase through CSS, but it is written in sentence case.

Do

Don't

Buttons and links

A button is a verb and its object, so it still makes sense read on its own. A link names where it goes. The button that confirms an action repeats that action's verb.

Do

Read the release notes

Don't

Click here for the notes.

Form labels

A label is a short noun phrase above its field, never a placeholder. Mark the optional fields with "(optional)", not the required ones, and put formats in the hint below.

Do

We only call about bookings.

Don't

Errors

Say what happened and how to fix it, in that order, next to the thing that went wrong. Name the field or the action, never the person. No codes unless someone will search for them, and then in mono after the words.

Do

That date has passed. Pick today or later.

Don't

You entered a wrong date.

Invalid input.

Empty states

Say what will be here and give the one action that fills it. If the person can't fill it (no results, nothing assigned yet), say why it's empty and what would change that.

Do

No trips yet

Plan one and it shows up here.

Don't

Nothing to see here!

Confirmations

Ask only before something that can't be undone. The title names the action and the object, the body says what goes with it, and the buttons are the verb and a way out ("Cancel", or "Keep trip" when that reads better). Where an action can be undone, do it and offer Undo instead of asking.

Do

Delete Lisbon 2027?

Its 12 photos go with it. This can't be undone.

Don't

Are you sure?

Success messages

Past tense, the object first, and Undo when there is one. A success message is quiet: a toast or an inline line, never a dialog.

Do

Trip deleted

Don't

Success!

Dates

In the interface a date is day, short month, year: "7 Nov 2026", with the weekday first when it helps planning ("Sat 7 Nov 2026"). Drop the year inside the current year when nothing is ambiguous. Data a person might copy, sort or search (logs, IDs, exports, timestamps in a table) uses ISO 8601 in --mono.

Do

Departs Sat 7 Nov 2026

2026-11-07 deploy ok

Don't

Departs 11/07/2026

Departs November 7th, 2026

Times

Twelve-hour, with a space and capital AM or PM; drop ":00" on the hour in prose. Add the zone when the reader might be elsewhere. 24-hour time is not used, except inside ISO 8601 data in mono (2026-11-07T21:30Z in a log). For something in the last day, a relative time ("5 min ago") is fine, with the full date and time in a tooltip or title.

Do

9:30 AM · 3 PM ET · 2–3 AM UTC

Don't

21:30 · 9:30am

Numbers

Numerals for every number, a comma every three digits, and a space before a unit except %. Compact durations in tables and mono data ("2h 11m", "150ms") can drop the space. Words a person typed, like a quoted message, stay as written. Figures that line up in a column use tabular figures, which the type tokens already set.

Do

3 trips · 10,482 photos · 64 GB · 64%

Don't

three trips · 10482 photos · 64GB · 64 %

Currency

The symbol before the number with no space. Whole amounts drop ".00" in prose; a table of prices shows two decimals on every row so they line up. Name the currency ("US$", "€") only when the reader could mistake it.

Do

$1,240 · $12.50

Don't

$1240.00 · 12.5 USD

What never appears

These don't appear in a product's interface: exclamation marks; "simply", "just" or "easy" when they play a task down (they blame the person when it isn't); "Oops" or "Uh-oh"; emoji as words; ALL CAPS typed into the text; and, on marketing pages, jargon the reader has to look up.

Do

Couldn't load your trips. Try again.

Don't

Oops! Something went wrong!! Simply refresh.
