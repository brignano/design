Pills, badges and tags

A pill says what state something is in, in a word. A badge counts. A tag is a filter you can take off. Colour marks a state you would act on, and categories stay neutral.

Use when

  • A row or a card needs its state in one or two words: .pill.
  • A button needs an unread count on it: .badge.
  • A list shows the filters applied, each removable: .tag.

Don't use when

  • A sentence of status: use a message.
  • A choice between filters: use checkboxes or the segmented control; tags only show the ones applied.
  • A count that is the whole point of the view: use a stat tile.

Pills

  • A bare .pill is neutral: drafts, categories, anything that asks nothing of the reader.
  • .pill-success, -attention, -danger and -info add a dot and their wash.
  • Map your own states onto these: booked is success, deposit due is attention, overdue is danger. Don't add a hue for a category.
  • Always a word. The dot backs up the word; it never replaces it.

Badges and tags

  • .badge pins a count to the corner of a .btn. Over 99, show "99+".
  • The badge is aria-hidden: put the count in the button's name ("Notifications, 3 unread") and update it with the count.
  • .tag is a filter chip; .tag-remove names what it removes. Your script removes the tag and moves focus to the next one, or to the filter input.

Accessibility

  • Pill text passes 4.5:1 on its wash in both themes; the dot passes 3:1.
  • The remove button is 24px, with 44px of hit area on touch.
  • Under forced colours the dot and the edges take the text colour.
