Use it

<nav class="breadcrumb" aria-label="Breadcrumb"><ol>
  <li><a href="/">Home</a></li>
  <li><a href="/work/x" aria-current="page">This page</a></li>
</ol></nav>

<nav class="pagination" aria-label="Pages">
  <a class="pagination-prev" href="?p=2">Previous</a>
  <ol class="pagination-pages"><li><a href="?p=3" aria-current="page">3</a></li></ol>
  <span class="pagination-status">Page 3 of 9</span>
  <a class="pagination-next" href="?p=4">Next</a>
</nav>

<div class="load-more">
  <span class="load-more-count" aria-live="polite">Showing 12 of 48 posts</span>
  <button type="button">Show 12 more</button>
</div>

Use when

  • Breadcrumb: pages sit two or more levels deep, like a case study inside Work.
  • Pagination: a table people page through and come back to ("page 3").
  • Load more: a content list people scan, like posts or photos.

Don't use when

  • Breadcrumb on a top-level page, or as the only navigation.
  • Numbered pages for a content list. Use load more.
  • Infinite scroll. It's not part of the system: people lose their place and the footer.

Accessibility

  • Both are a <nav> with an aria-label, so they appear in the landmarks list.
  • The current crumb and the current page carry aria-current="page"; the separators and arrows are drawn by CSS and not read aloud.
  • A missing previous or next is a <span aria-disabled="true">, not a link that goes nowhere.
  • After load more, move focus to the first new item so keyboard users don't start over.
  • Put aria-live="polite" on .load-more-count and update its text, so screen readers hear "Showing 24 of 48 posts" when the new items arrive.
  • With more than one pagination on a page, give each its own label, like aria-label="Pages, deployments", so the landmarks stay distinct.
