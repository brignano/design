Wireframe: each product page looks like its product

Today Driftwood, Guidebook and Sites share a header, a ground, a typeface, a blue button, a status pill and a rounded stage. Only a tint and left or right change, so flipping between them reads as one site. Below, the first screen of each is drawn from what the product does, at phone and desktop width.

Each product picks five things, and no two pick the same:

  • Ground: the page's own surface (always dark, bright white, a warm wash of the mark).
  • Voice: how the headline is set (mono, heavy 800, soft medium).
  • Shape: square, pill or soft corners, on the button and everything else.
  • Composition: where the product sits (it is the background, it annotates the page, it leans in from the edge).
  • The page does the thing: one piece of the product's own interface, used on the page itself.

What stays shared: the five-line brief, show then tell, one primary action, the first-phone-screen check, the tokens, and the A|B stamp in the footer. That stamp is the only family resemblance.

What this breaks on purpose: the primary button is no longer cobalt on a product page (it takes the mark, or ink). Inside the apps it stays cobalt. This needs your yes: it reverses part of ADR-0011.

Rejected: more knobs on the current template (a fourth layout, stronger tint): the sameness is the shared header, button and stage, which knobs don't touch. A different typeface per product: the strongest lever, but three more fonts to load and license; try this first.

1. Driftwood: the incident console

Always dark, all mono, square corners. The system map is the page's background, with the broken box in red. The headline is the added line of a diff. The header is a status line, not a nav.
