Wireframe: design v1.0

The plan for design v1.0 (#171), drawn: a house look every product shares, a theme per product so they don't look alike, a new home for this site, and the three kits the themes are built from. Every colour, typeface, corner and icon stroke is a candidate token; nothing here ships.

Rejected before drawing: one look for every app (they would look alike again); a dark-only "console" house (it suits Driftwood, not Sites); keeping Geist with a new accent (the "evolve" option turned down on 2026-11-21); glass and gradients (dates fast, costs contrast).

Five products, one system

Anthony's calls on 2026-11-22: one house look plus a theme for each product; then, on seeing the first drawing (every product on the same sidebar, stats and table), "still really cookie cutter". He was right: a theme changed the skin and nothing else. So each product's main screen is now built around the one thing that product does, on a shell of its own.

  • The house (shared by all): how every control behaves, mono for data, the product | section bar above each heading, the A|B stamp, and the accessibility floor.
  • A theme (per product): a kit, a ground, an action hue, a typeface, corners, and a shell. The whole file is printed above each product.
  • A shell is the shape of the main screen: a console of tiles, a canvas, a player, a conversation, a document. The system ships the shells; a product picks one.
  • Driftwood's diff, Guidebook's counts and all of Receipts are demo data. Sites and homelab use the words and data already in this repo.

Rejected: the same sidebar, four stats and a table in five colours (the first drawing; it is what "cookie cutter" described).

The design site's new home

Today's home is 40 identical cards. This one opens on the system doing its job: tap a product and the screen re-themes, shape and all, then build a theme of your own and read the file it makes. It works here: try the chips, the slider and the two menus.

Rejected: a longer card grid with filters (still a catalogue); an animated hero with no controls (shows nothing true about the system).

The three kits

The looks the themes above are built from, each on Sites' product page and homelab's Hosts view. Split is the house and the default kit; Ledger and Larch are the two alternatives a product can start from.

1 of 3Ledger

A well-kept notebook. Warm paper, a serif for headings, hairline rules instead of cards and shadows. Ink does the acting: the primary button is black, and amber is a highlighter, used once a screen.

  • Type: Newsreader (headings and big figures), Instrument Sans (interface), IBM Plex Mono (data).
  • Shape: 3px corners, ruled tables with a double rule on top, outlined stamps for status. No shadows.
  • Icons: 1.5px stroke, flat ends, sharp joins.

2 of 3Split

The A|B bar becomes the system. Black and white with one signal colour, wide heavy capitals for headings, mono capitals for labels, and a vertical bar wherever two things sit side by side: a label and its value, a place and its page, a status and its word.

  • Type: Archivo, one variable file used at two widths (wide for headings, normal for text), and Martian Mono for labels and data.
  • Shape: square corners, 1px ink borders, no shadows. Selected means inverted.
  • Colour: a lime signal fills the one primary action and marks the one highlight. Links and text stay ink.
  • Icons: 1.5px stroke, square ends, sharp joins.

3 of 3Larch

The names already in the tokens (larch, forest, rust) made visible. Cream ground, surfaces separated by tint rather than borders, forest green for anything you can act on, amber for the one highlight. Soft and friendly.

  • Type: Bricolage Grotesque (headings and big figures), Onest (interface), IBM Plex Mono (data).
  • Shape: pill buttons, 20px cards, no borders. Night mode is deep green, not black.
  • Icons: 1.75px stroke, round ends, with a soft fill when selected.
